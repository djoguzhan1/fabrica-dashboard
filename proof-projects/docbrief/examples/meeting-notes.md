# Warehouse Scanner Rollout — Steering Meeting, 14 March

Attendees: operations lead, IT lead, finance, two shift supervisors. Minutes taken by the IT lead.
(Fictional sample document used to demonstrate docbrief. All names, figures and dates are made up.)

## 1. Where we are

The pilot at the Leeds site has run for six weeks with 18 handheld scanners on the day shift.
Pick accuracy went from 97.1% to 99.4% and average pick time per line dropped from 41 seconds
to 33 seconds. Two scanners were returned with cracked screens; both were replaced under
warranty within four working days.

Battery life is the main complaint. The vendor's spec says 12 hours; on the floor we see
roughly 8.5 hours, which does not cover a 10-hour shift without a swap. Supervisors have been
managing this with a mid-shift battery swap at the charging cabinet, which costs each picker
about three minutes.

## 2. Costs so far

Hardware for the pilot came to £14,200 (18 units at £690 plus the charging cabinet at £1,780).
Software licences are £11 per device per month. Training took 1.5 hours per picker and was
delivered by the two supervisors, so there was no external cost. Finance confirmed the pilot
finished £900 under the approved budget of £16,000.

## 3. Options for the rollout

Three options were discussed for the remaining four sites (Bristol, Glasgow, Nottingham,
Swansea), which together run 112 pickers across two shifts.

Option A — full rollout in one wave in May. Needs 130 scanners (112 plus 18 spares) and five
charging cabinets. Estimated hardware cost £98,600. Fastest payback but the IT team would need
two contractors for four weeks.

Option B — two waves: Bristol and Glasgow in May, Nottingham and Swansea in August. Same total
hardware cost, spread over two quarters. Lets us fix the battery issue before the second wave.

Option C — delay everything until the vendor ships the higher-capacity battery, expected Q4.
No cost this year, but we keep the current accuracy gap at four sites for another six months.

The operations lead prefers Option B. Finance can support either A or B within the current
year's capital plan but asked for a written comparison before the 28 March budget meeting.

## 4. Decisions

- Proceed with Option B, subject to finance sign-off on 28 March.
- Buy the vendor's extended battery pack (£38 each) for all day-shift devices in wave one and
  measure whether it closes the gap to a full 10-hour shift.
- Keep the mid-shift swap procedure as the fallback and document it in the SOP.

## 5. Actions

- IT lead: send the Option A vs B comparison to finance by 21 March.
- Operations lead: confirm picker headcount per site with HR by 19 March.
- Supervisors: write up the battery swap procedure as a one-page SOP by 25 March.
- Finance: confirm capital allocation at the 28 March meeting.
- IT lead: raise a support ticket with the vendor about the 8.5-hour battery figure and ask for
  a firm date on the higher-capacity battery.

## 6. Open points

Nobody could confirm whether the Swansea site has enough Wi-Fi coverage in the far racking
area; a site survey was requested but not yet scheduled. It is also unclear whether the
